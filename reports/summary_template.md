realism bands: Four ranges that a model run on the APL16 panel has to stay inside: its response rate with APL on, the ratio of its response rates with APL off and with APL on, its response rate ρ with APL on, and its distribution of the number of odors per KC. baseline-noise units: A response divided by the unit's own noise before the odor, the standard deviation of its signal within the pre-odor frames. The boutons, the claws and the KC somata are counted in these units.

APL-effect loss: The root mean squared difference, over the 120 odor pairs of the APL16 panel, between the model's and the flies' per-pair APL effect: the distance between two odors with APL on minus their distance with APL off. The main target of the earlier work, now a test on recordings that a fit to the stages never sees. Lower is better.

## What is recorded: the stages, the panels and the flies {#recordings}

This page follows one target. A model of the fly's olfactory pathway is held to the responses that were recorded at every stage of that pathway: the olfactory receptor neurons (ORNs), the projection neurons (PNs) at their dendrites and at their boutons, and the Kenyon cells (KCs) of the mushroom body at their claws and at their somata. The model and the flies are compared as populations, stage by stage.

The earlier work held models to what one inhibitory neuron of the mushroom body, APL, does to the distances between odors at the KCs. It is on the page "APL16 Summary". What carries over from it is [near the end of this page](#apl16-carryover).

### Three views of a population, and what the flies' views look like {#views}

**A population** is the table of trial-mean responses of all units of one stage to the odors of one panel. A bouton region has no glomerulus label, a claw region is not assigned to a KC, and a recorded KC cannot be matched to a model KC. So a model is compared with the flies on what does not depend on which unit is which.

**Three views of a population** are read, the same at every stage. They are the three rows of the figure above.

- **Strength by odor:** each odor's response rate (the fraction of units that respond to it) and each odor's mean response, both as multiples of their mean over the panel's odors.
- **Sharing between odors:** for each pair of odors, the cosine distance between their responses across the units, once on which units respond and once on how strongly the responders respond.
- **Breadth among units:** the distribution of the number of odors that a unit responds to.

Where the units are named glomeruli, at the ORNs and the PN dendrites, each glomerulus's response to each odor is read as a fourth view.

**The odors' strengths are re-ordered along the pathway.** aa is among the weakest odors at the PN dendrites, at {{E138/stages.csv:panel=megamat;condition=on;group=family_fitted;stage=pn_dendrite;row=common;odor=aa:rate_flies ~ .2f}} of the panel's mean response rate, and among the strongest at the KC somata, at {{E138/stages.csv:panel=megamat;condition=on;group=family_fitted;stage=kc_soma;row=common;odor=aa:rate_flies ~ .2f}}. B-cit goes the other way, from {{E138/stages.csv:panel=megamat;condition=on;group=family_fitted;stage=kc_claw;row=common;odor=B-cit:rate_flies ~ .2f}} at the claws to {{E138/stages.csv:panel=megamat;condition=on;group=family_fitted;stage=kc_soma;row=common;odor=B-cit:rate_flies ~ .2f}} at the somata. A model therefore cannot get the somata's strengths right by handing on the strengths of its input.

**The KC somata hold odors apart, and their cells respond to few odors.** At the ORNs nearly every glomerulus responds to many of the odors, and two odors share most of the glomeruli that respond. At the KC somata most cells respond to no odor or to one, and two odors share few of the cells that respond. The stages between lie between (the middle and bottom rows of the figure).

## How a model is compared with the flies {#target}

### One-fly units and the floor {#one-fly-units}

**One-fly units.** A view's error is the root mean squared difference between the model's view and the flies' mean view, over the view's entries: the odors, the pairs or the bins. It is divided by the same difference for one fly against the mean of the other flies. A value of 1 says that the model is as far from the flies as one fly is from the others, and lower is better ([how it is computed](#fn-one-fly)).

**The floor** is what a resampled set of the same flies gives against the measured set. No model can be expected below it. **The most atypical fly** is the fly furthest from the mean of the others, and it sets the line for "inside" ([below](#gating)).

**The room for a model is between the floor and one fly.** By construction one fly reads about 1. The floors are near a third at every stage, so a model at 1 has about two thirds of a one-fly unit left to gain.

### Like for like {#like-for-like}

Both sides are read the same way at every stage, and a stage's reading is fixed before any model is read on it.

- **The flies' responses** are counted in units of their own noise, with one response criterion per stage ([footnote](#fn-criteria)).
- **The model's units are sampled and pooled as the recording was made:** the glomeruli that the driver line labels, and {{lit:14 # results/E134_population_baseline/lock.json, readings.constants.CLAW_REGION_SIZE}} claws to a claw region.
- **The model's values pass through one monotone curve per stage,** set on the pooled values and never on anything about odors. An indicator's unknown curve is then neither counted as error nor used to hide one ([footnote](#fn-stage-curve)).
- **The model is given the flies' trial noise.**

Every stage has a second reading as well, with another curve, another pooling or raw spike counts. A stage whose verdict changes between the two is "not readable" and stays out of the comparison until a reading holds.

### The numbers that are compared {#numbers}

- **The population error** of a stage is the root mean square of its views' errors, each in one-fly units: one number per stage, panel and condition.
- **The megamat stage error** is the root mean square of the errors of four megamat stages: the PN dendrites, the PN boutons, the KC claws and the KC somata. The ORN stage is the model's input, so it is read and not counted.
- **The megamat stage error on strength by odor** takes each stage's strength error alone. It is the number that models are chosen and gated on, for the reason of the next subsection.

**Strength by odor passes, and nothing else built from the views does.** The error over the three views is just under the line with APL on and well under it with APL off. Sharing between odors fails with APL off, and breadth fails in both conditions. Strength and sharing together fall short with APL off. So strength by odor alone chooses and gates models.

**No model is chosen by a correlation.** ρ on odor-odor distances clears the line and response rate ρ does not. Neither sees a view's level or its spread, so neither is a candidate. Both are still reported.

**Why breadth cannot order the models.** Once a model's values pass through the stage's curve, its response rate, and with it most of its breadth, is the flies'. What is left differs less between the variants than a resampled set of flies moves: the middle half of the variants' breadth errors spans {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_breadth.csv:reading=curve_noise;condition=on;measure=breadth:iqr ~ .2f}} with APL on, against a floor of {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_breadth.csv:reading=curve_noise;condition=on;measure=breadth:flies_floor ~ .2f}}.

**What sharing and breadth add beside it.** They are reported for every model at every stage, with the error over all three views. They say where a model is furthest from the flies, as leads. And they guard a gain: a change that lowers the strength error and raises another view's error by more than that error's spread over the wirings is not an improvement.

### Choosing and gating {#gating}

- **An improvement** lowers the megamat stage error on strength by odor, on wirings and flies that the change was not fitted to, by more than the wirings' spread. It raises no stage's error and no held-out panel's by more than that stage's own spread.
- **A model is read as a population of flies.** The best family exists on eight wirings, each read as one fly, and a model's value is the mean over its wirings. Fits use four wirings and half of each stage's flies, and are read on the four other wirings against the other half.
- **A stage is "inside"** if the model is no further from the flies' mean than the most atypical fly is from the other flies, on {{lit:six # E134_population_baseline.md, Design, as run, The classes, fixed beforehand: inside on 6 of 8 wirings}} of the eight wirings. It is "far" if it is above {{lit:2 # E134_population_baseline.md, Design, as run, The classes, fixed beforehand: far if above 2 on 6 of 8}} on as many.
- **A view's, a stage's or a single odor's share of an error is a lead,** never a result. Near-ties are confirmed on held-out flies, wirings or panels.
- **Realism is a constraint, not a target.** The four realism bands hold for every model run on the APL16 panel ([footnote](#fn-realism-bands)), and every section names what would be hard to defend in a fly.

### The held-out panels read like for like {#heldout-readings}

**The question was whether the baseline's verdicts on the held-out panels are the model's or the reading's.** The baseline read the mixture flies from one uploaded value per cell and stimulus, and validation2 on part of its flies and odors. In the held-out panels read like for like, the family's saved runs are scored against the flies read in several ways. No model was run.

### The antennal lobe held to the recorded PN dendrites {#antennal-lobe-dendrites}

**The question was whether the megamat stage error is made before the calyx.** In the antennal lobe held to the recorded PN dendrites, the model's PN stage is held to the recorded dendrites in two ways. It is fitted to them, and the recorded dendrites themselves are put in its place ("imposed"). The family's calyx stays as it is, with both response rates held again.

**Nothing carries past the dendrites, so the megamat stage error is not made before the calyx.** With the recorded dendrites imposed, the dendrites come nearer the flies, and the boutons, the claws and the somata all move away, on every wiring. The fitted stages bring the boutons nearer and move the claws and the somata further away. With the family's calyx as it is, a PN stage nearer the flies' is a worse input to it.

**"Imposed" keeps the flies' arrangement and the model's rates.** On the {{lit:28 # results/E135_antennal_lobe_dendrites/lock.json, glomeruli: the 28 that the ORN recordings, the PN-dendrite recordings and the model's input all hold}} glomeruli that the recordings and the model's input share, each PN's rate is set from the flies' mean dendrite response, rank for rank on the model's own rates. The dendrites enter in units of the flies' own noise, and once more as the change in fluorescence over its baseline (dF/F).

## Caveats {#caveats}

**The mixture sessions have a fixed order, so the kiwi mixtures gate nothing.** In a mixture session the components are given first, the mixtures after them, and in most flies the components once more.

- **Every presentation of an odor evokes less than the one before.** The components' second block evokes {{E137_heldout_readings/position_decline.csv:fly=2022-07-11/2;units=noise:component_rate_later_over_first_block ~ .2f}} to {{E137_heldout_readings/position_decline.csv:fly=2022-07-12/3;units=noise:component_rate_later_over_first_block ~ .2f}} of their first block's response rate in the kiwi flies (the median per fly).
- **The fall follows an odor's own repeats, not the time in the session.** In the megamat sessions, whose order is drawn anew for each block, an odor's response rate falls by a factor of {{E137_heldout_readings/component_fall/megamat_repeats.csv:fly=2022-10-10/1:rate_factor_per_repeat ~ .2f}} to {{E137_heldout_readings/component_fall/megamat_repeats.csv:fly=2022-11-10/1:rate_factor_per_repeat ~ .2f}} per repeat in three of the four flies, where as many trials of other odors cost a factor of {{E137_heldout_readings/component_fall/megamat_repeats.csv:fly=2022-11-10/1:rate_factor_per_block_of_places ~ .2f}} to {{E137_heldout_readings/component_fall/megamat_repeats.csv:fly=2022-10-10/2:rate_factor_per_block_of_places ~ .2f}}.
- **So no reading sets fresh mixtures against fresh components.** The mixtures come after trials of their own components, while the components' first block is fresh, and the model has no adaptation. Sessions with the mixtures first or interleaved would settle it, and they would be new recordings.

**What was decided for the mixture panels.**

- **The kiwi mixtures leave the test of whether a fit holds.** They are reported beside it, with the readings against the components' first block and against their second block as bounds. No fit is gated on them.
- **The control mixtures stay in the test,** read as uploaded, with the second-block reading beside. The family is inside on them under every reading, so their class does not turn on the choice.
- **Megamat and validation2 are not touched.** Their order is drawn anew for each block and fly, so the fall enters the flies' spread and not their mean. The order of the APL16 recordings is not in their processed tables.

**Validation2 has been read once, for a regression.** The test of a common cause behind the odors' strength errors was judged on validation2's odors ([above](#strength-entries)). No class of glomeruli passed, so nothing is built on its result. Still, validation2 is no longer an unseen panel for anything chosen from those classes or from those readings: a change proposed from them has to be checked on other held-out odors or flies. For fits that take nothing from that test, validation2 stays held out.

**Further limits of the readings.**

- **The flies are few after the dendrites:** {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_stage_table.csv:group=megamat_fitted;stage=pn_bouton:n_flies ~ w}} bouton flies, {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_stage_table.csv:group=megamat_fitted;stage=kc_claw:n_flies ~ w}} claw flies and {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_stage_table.csv:group=megamat_fitted;stage=kc_soma:n_flies ~ w}} soma flies, so a half of a stage's flies is two flies. - **"Inside" is a loose line.** It is measured against the most atypical fly, and a model that is inside can still be most of a one-fly unit above the floor.
- **The measure's consistency is shown at one stage of one panel,** the KC somata of the APL16 panel, since the saved variants hold no other. At the megamat stages it is read with the fit of all megamat stages ([pending work](#pending)).
- **The equal weights are a convention.** The megamat stage error weighs its four stages alike, and a population error its views. Every stage's and every view's error is given beside a combined number for that reason.
- **The stages are different flies, indicators and compartments.** The PN-dendrite recordings hold {{lit:28 # results/E135_antennal_lobe_dendrites/lock.json, glomeruli: the 28 that the ORN recordings, the PN-dendrite recordings and the model's input all hold}} of the model's {{lit:36 # E135_antennal_lobe_dendrites.md, Caveats: the imposed table is set on 28 of the model's 36 input glomeruli}} input glomeruli, and the bouton and claw regions carry no labels.

## Footnotes {#footnotes}

### How one-fly units and the floor are computed {#fn-one-fly}

Each view has one or two parts. Strength by odor has the odors' response rates and the odors' mean responses. Sharing between odors has the cosine distance on which units respond and on how strongly the responders respond. Breadth among units has one part, and so has the glomerulus view.

- **A part's one-fly unit** is the root mean squared distance of one fly's part from the mean part of the other flies, over the flies and the part's entries.
- **A part's error** for a model is the root mean squared difference between the model's part and the flies' mean part, over the entries, divided by that unit.
- **A view's error** is the root mean square of its parts' errors, and a population error is the root mean square of its views' errors.
- **The floor** is the mean error of {{lit:1,000 # N_BOOT in kcq/population_fit.py}} sets of the same flies, drawn with replacement, each scored as a model is against the measured set.
- **One fly against the others** is each fly's error against the mean of the other flies, in the units of all the flies.

A unit from four flies is itself uncertain. In the fit of all megamat stages a half of a stage's flies can be two flies. There the one-fly units of all the stage's flies are kept, since two flies give one difference as their unit.

### The monotone curve per stage, and the two readings of a stage {#fn-stage-curve}

A calcium indicator's signal is some rising function of a unit's activity, and that function is not known. So a model's values at a stage are not compared with the flies' as they are.

- **The curve.** The model's pooled values, over every unit and odor of the stage, are mapped onto the flies' pooled values rank for rank, at {{lit:201 # results/E134_population_baseline/lock.json, readings.constants.N_CURVE_KNOTS}} evenly spaced quantiles. The curve is non-decreasing and is set for each wiring, with APL on.
- **What it cannot do.** It is set on two pooled distributions, with no odor, unit or pair label. A permutation of either side gives the same curve, so it cannot move an odor's strength relative to another's except through the values themselves.
- **The noise.** After the curve the model's values get the flies' trial noise: the spread of single trials about their odor means, averaged over as many trials as the flies were given.
- **The criterion.** A model unit then responds if its value is above the flies' criterion of the stage.

Every stage has a second reading fixed beforehand: a straight line in place of the curve, another pooling of the boutons or the claws, or at the KC somata raw spike counts with two or more spikes as a response. A stage whose class differs between its two readings is "not readable".

### The response criterion at each stage {#fn-criteria}

A response of the flies is counted in units of noise, so that an indicator's scale does not enter.

- **At the ORNs and the PN dendrites** the unit is the spread of single trials about their odor means, and a glomerulus responds above {{E134_population_baseline/flies_views.csv:population=orn__megamat__wild_type;name=strength:criterion ~ .0f}} of those units.
- **At the boutons and the claws** the unit is the noise before the odor, the standard deviation of the region's signal within the pre-odor frames, and a region responds above {{E134_population_baseline/flies_views.csv:population=pn_bouton__megamat__wild_type;name=strength:criterion ~ .0f}} of those units.
- **At the KC somata** the unit is the same, and the level is set per set of recordings. For the APL16 panel it is {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_soma_criteria.csv:cohort=apl16, control flies:criterion ~ .2f}}, the level at which the control flies keep the response rate of their experimenter's own criterion, {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_soma_criteria.csv:cohort=apl16, control flies:rate_mean ~ .3f}}.
- **The megamat and validation2 somata** take {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_soma_criteria.csv:cohort=megamat, Remy's four flies:criterion ~ .2f}}, the level at which the megamat soma flies keep the response rate of their experimenter's own criterion, {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_soma_criteria.csv:cohort=megamat, Remy's four flies:rate_mean ~ .4f}}. At the APL16 panel's level they would respond at {{E134_population_baseline/writeup_soma_criteria.csv:cohort=megamat, Remy's four flies:rate_mean_at_apl16_criterion ~ .3f}}.
- **The mixture somata as uploaded** are in each cell's z-score over its session, with a level set the same way. The readings by position in the session are in baseline-noise units.

A model's KC responds on raw spike counts if it fires two or more spikes to the odor.

### The realism bands {#fn-realism-bands}

A model run on the APL16 panel is flagged if it leaves one of four ranges. The model's side is counted on raw spike counts, two or more spikes as a response, and the flies' side in baseline-noise units.

### The APL-effect statistics {#fn-apl-effect}

For each of the 120 odor pairs of the APL16 panel, the per-pair APL effect is the distance between the two odors' KC responses with APL on minus their distance with APL off. In the flies that is the control flies' distance minus the distance with APL output blocked (TNT), each the mean over the recordings of the cosine distance. In a model it is taken from one run with APL on and one with APL's output weights at zero.

- **The APL-effect loss** is the root mean squared difference between the model's and the flies' per-pair effect. Its floor is the loss of a resampled set of the recordings against the measured set.
- **The mean APL effect** is the mean over the pairs, and **the pattern part** is the loss with both means removed.
- **The pattern part splits exactly** into an amplitude part, how much wider or narrower the model spreads its effect over the pairs than the flies do, and a shape part, which is zero when the model's effect is the flies' stretched about its mean.
- **The two correlations** over the pairs, ρ and Pearson r, are descriptions. No model is chosen by a correlation: neither sees the mean or the spread of the effect.

On this page the statistics are read through the calibrated readout, a saturating function of a model KC's spike count whose half-saturation is set on the model's own counts with APL on. The fits that made the family, the APL-effect fits and the APL-effect fit on re-drawn wirings, and every other reading of these statistics are on the page "APL16 Summary".
