re-drawn: Of the PN→KC wiring: a randomized version of the hemibrain wiring that the model otherwise uses, made either by permuting the claws among the KCs of each type or by a shuffle that keeps every glomerulus pair's count of shared KCs. Each KC keeps its number of claws and each glomerulus its number of claws; which KC gets which glomerulus changes. A model on a re-drawn wiring stands for another fly with the same statistics, so a result that holds on re-drawn wirings does not depend on the one mapped brain.
APL effect: Of one odor pair, its odor-odor distance with APL on minus its distance with APL off. In the KC data, the control flies' distance minus the distance with APL output blocked (TNT), each the mean over recordings of the cosine distance; in a model, its distance with APL on minus its distance with APL acutely off.
APL-effect loss: The root mean squared difference between a model's and the KC data's per-pair APL effect over the 120 odor pairs; lower is better. It splits exactly into a mean part and a pattern part, and its floor is the loss that a second sample of the same flies would show against the first.
mean part: The part of the APL-effect loss that comes from the model's mean APL effect over the 120 pairs differing from the flies'.
pattern part: The APL-effect loss with both means removed: how far the model's pattern of the APL effect across the 120 pairs is from the flies', whatever the two means are. Since 2026-11-13 it is the measure on which variants are compared.
amplitude part: The part of the pattern part that comes from the model spreading its per-pair APL effect over the pairs more widely or more narrowly than the flies do. Its square and the square of the shape part add up to the squared pattern part.
shape part: The part of the pattern part that is left once the two spreads are accounted for. It is zero when the model's per-pair APL effect is the flies' stretched or squeezed about its mean.
spread of the effect: The standard deviation of a per-pair APL effect across the 120 pairs, for a model as a multiple of the flies'. Above one, APL moves the model's pairs apart more unevenly than it moves the flies'.
common error: The part of the best family's pattern error that every wiring shares: the per-pair APL effect of the mean over eight wirings minus the KC data's, each side's mean removed, under the calibrated readout. In it the model's APL effect is too small among 2h, IaA, pa, eb, ep and ms and too large among 1-8ol, 1-5ol, 1-6ol and 6al.
calibrated readout: Each model spike count n replaced by n / (n + k) before the distances are taken, with k set on the model's counts with APL on so that an odor's response is spread over the responding KCs as in the control flies. It compresses large counts as a calcium indicator does and leaves which KCs respond, and every response rate, unchanged.
firing ceiling: In a model, a refractory period after each spike together with an adaptation that raises the KC's threshold at every spike and decays slowly, which bounds how many spikes a KC fires to one odor.
realism bands: The four ranges that a model has to stay inside for its loss to count: its response rate with APL on, the ratio of its response rates with APL off and on, its response rate ρ with APL on, and its distribution of the number of odors each KC responds to, each set from the recordings.
baseline-noise units: A KC's response divided by the standard deviation of its own signal within the pre-odor windows of its recording. Unlike the recordings' z-scored unit, it does not grow with how much the KC responds. The realism bands count the flies' responses in these units.
oracle: A model manipulation that is handed part of the answer (which glomeruli or which KCs to change, read off the KC data or off the model's output scored against them). A ceiling test, never a mechanism.
breadth-2 family: The best family on the main target: APL inhibition weighted by how broadly each KC is driven (breadth exponent 2), KC–KC inhibition, no per-claw threshold and no glomerulus gain, fitted to the APL effect at the control flies' response rate. Its fits on eight wirings, the real one and seven re-drawn, are the reference of every experiment since.
block: The fruity × small-ester block: the nine pairs of a fruity ester (2h, IaA, pa) with a small ester (2-but, eb, ep), which the control flies' KCs hold further apart than the ORN input predicts and which close when APL output is blocked.
co-response: Of two odors, how many times more often than chance a KC that responds to one responds to the other.
draw of the wiring: One re-drawn version of the PN→KC wiring (a permutation of claws within KC types, or a shuffle matched to the real wiring on every glomerulus pair's count of shared KCs). The standard deviation of one family's loss over such draws, each with its own fit, is the yardstick for whether two fits differ.
wirings of the earlier searches: The real wiring and three re-drawn ones, on which a change of the best family is run first. The family's other four wirings are held out, for a setting that is chosen on the first four.
margin of an equal: The band inside which a changed model counts as level with the unchanged fits: a pattern part within 0.003 of theirs against all recordings and against both halves of the flies, inside the realism bands, with limits on the loss on raw counts and on the mean APL effect.
bouton path: The second of the two paths by which the model's APL acts: a subtraction from the rate of every PN bouton, which every claw on that bouton receives. The first path is APL's inhibition of the KCs at each claw.
weightings' line: For a model, the ester pairs' mean distance with APL on relative to the mean over all 120 pairs, set against the same for the alcohol pairs. Over thirteen breadth weightings of APL the two fall on one line, and the control flies lie far off it.
step from claws to somata: Of two odors, the distance between their responses across the KC somata minus that across the KC claws, each one minus the Pearson correlation across the units of the stage. A positive step means that the somata hold the two odors further apart than the claws do.
settled level: The level of a unit of the model before the odor, taken over the last quarter of a second before it. The simulation starts the KCs, their claws and APL at zero half a second before the odor, and a mean over that whole half second reads the claws and the boutons high and APL low.
lab's input: The model's ORN input: the lab's ORN recordings for the 36 glomeruli that both of its panels hold. The wiring's other 18 glomeruli receive zeros.
fuller input: The lab's input with measured responses for ten of the 18 glomeruli that it leaves at zero.
base with the flies' recruitment: An oracle version of the breadth-2 family in which each odor's response rate is set to the flies', with APL on and with APL off.
read in proportion: Of the model's APL activity, compared with the flies' calcium signal without a nonlinearity for the indicator. A class that is the same through a family of steeper indicator nonlinearities is called held.
leak: In the dual-colour recordings, the part of APL's green signal that appears in the red channel of the KCs.
held-out series: A series of recordings of APL alone from 2021 (five flies), made as a control with three vials of one odor in every recording, and read by rules that were written before any of its maps was opened.
on hold: Since 2026-11-15, tables of APL's synapses, PN boutons and KC claws derived from four connectome data sets in separate work are not to be used until they are cleared, and no number that rests on them is given here. The hemibrain tables that the model itself reads are not affected.

## In brief {#brief}

**Since the main goal became the APL effect on odor-odor distances, pair by pair, the mean of that effect has been accounted for and its pattern across pairs has not.** The models' mean effect was too small mostly because raw spike counts were being compared with a calcium signal, and because about twenty over-driven model KCs dominated the distances: read through a compressive readout, or with a ceiling on how many spikes a KC fires, the best family has the flies' mean effect inside the realism bands.

**What is left is an error in the pattern that every wiring shares, too little APL effect among the esters and too much among the alcohols, well above the floor that the recordings' own variability sets, and it has not moved since the family was fitted.** Every kind of change to the model that was tried against it ended in one of three ways. It left the pattern part of the loss where it was. Or it made the pattern part worse. Or it lowered the pattern part on the wirings and the recordings that it was fitted to, and little or none of that gain was left on wirings and recordings that it had not been fitted to. The one change whose gain did hold there lowered the pattern part by {{E86/gate.csv:change=gate:g ~ .4f}}, where the pattern part stands at {{E72/population.csv:group=all:cal_pattern ~ .3f}} and its floor is {{E66/data_floor_parts.csv:data=graded;stat=apl_effect_rmse_centered:mean ~ .3f}}.

**The input is closed in its fitted entries, in its spread between flies and in its coverage.** APL's side of the best family is closed at both of its sites: at the KCs in the weighting, in the place where the inhibition is summed and in its form, and at the PN boutons in strength and in form. What those experiments added is a description of the family, with its prices: for a KC its APL is close to a fixed raise of the threshold while APL is on, most of it made at the PN boutons, where the connectome puts about a tenth of APL's calyx synapses, and holding that path near the connectome's share costs three to nine thousandths of the pattern part.

**Read the same way on both sides, the model with APL on holds the ester pairs too close together and the alcohol pairs too far apart, and no re-weighting, summation or gain of APL's inhibition moves the two groups in opposite directions.**

**Recordings of APL itself have been read against the model for the first time.** Along concentration the family's APL keeps its strength where the flies' APL loses half of it. The flatness is made in the model's antennal lobe, and a normalization weak enough to give the family the flies' fall costs about five thousandths of the pattern part at fixed scales and takes the KCs' responses at the weakest inputs further from the flies'; the family fitted again with it is in hand. Across odors the flies' APL does not at present constrain the family, since the series of recordings disagree and a ratio between two odors read from one pair of vials is known to no better than a factor of about one and a half.

**Under the goal before it, the fit of the two distance matrices, the gap was traced to a few odors whose modelled input collapses onto the same KCs, which an ORN measurement at the recordings' concentrations can settle and a fit cannot.**

- **The mean of the APL effect is matched, the pattern is not.** The table under [where the main target stands](#standing) gives both, with the pattern part split into its amplitude part and its shape part, as the rules of 2026-11-13 on [how the main target is read](#reading) ask.
- **The best family is a family and not one wiring.** Fitted again on seven re-drawn wirings it ends around the same loss, and what all eight wirings get wrong is the same.
- **Nothing tried has lowered that common error by more than a trace on wirings and recordings that it was not fitted to.** Two tables under [what was tried against the common error](#attempts) hold the kinds of change tried up to 2026-11-13, each brought up to date, and what each experiment since then did to the main target.
- **The best family's APL has been described and held against the flies' APL,** under [what the best family's APL is](#family-apl) and [the family's APL against the flies' APL](#flies-apl).
- **The recordings were read more closely than before,** and several findings are about the data and not the model: [what the recordings themselves showed](#recordings). Since 2026-11-15 a set of connectome tables is on hold, and what that withdrew from the earlier version of this page is under [what is withheld](#hold).
- **A list of choices is open,** and several questions are with the experimenters: [what is open](#open).

### How the main target is read since 2026-11-13 {#reading}

**The goal did not change on 2026-11-13; how it is read did.** The APL-effect loss stays the quantity to improve, inside the realism bands. Five rules, in force for every proposal, test and write-up since 2026-11-13, say how variants are compared on it.

- **The pattern part decides, and a correlation never does.** Where variants are compared on the pattern of the effect, within an experiment, in a test or as the best of a table, the measure is the pattern part, read with the loss and the mean APL effect beside it. No variant is chosen, tested or called best by ρ on the per-pair APL effect or by any other correlation, since a correlation sees neither the mean nor the spread of a model's effect.
- **The pattern part is given with its two parts.** Every pattern part that a conclusion rests on is given with its amplitude part, its shape part and the spread of the effect as a multiple of the flies'. Neither part is a measure to choose by.
- **The pairs are not weighted** by how well the recordings measure them.
- **A correlation is a description.** ρ on the per-pair APL effect is still quoted, with its ceiling, and Pearson r on the per-pair APL effect goes beside it where the two parts are shown.
- **Per-odor readings of the error are leads,** never results, and no mechanism is proposed on a single odor's share of an error without a check on halves of the recordings.

The evidence is how consistently each statistic orders the saved model variants: every distinct saved variant was scored, on raw spike counts, against two random halves of the recordings.

## Under the first goal: where the gap in ρ on odor-odor distances lies {#first-goal}

**The gap is on a few odors, and not where the fits had been scored.** ρ on odor-odor distances is one number over 120 pairs, and the per-pair decomposition of the gap split one minus ρ exactly into a share for each pair and each odor. The nine block pairs and the pairs within a chemical group, on which the fits until then had been scored, carry a tenth of the gap. Three odors carry two thirds of it: paa, B-myr and 2-but, which every model puts far too close to one another. The reference model has paa and B-myr at a cosine distance of {{E61/worst_pairs.csv:short=reference 40 Hz;condition=on;pair=paa / B-myr:model_distance ~ .2f}} with APL on, where the control flies have {{E61/worst_pairs.csv:short=reference 40 Hz;condition=on;pair=paa / B-myr:data_distance ~ .2f}}, nearly orthogonal. The three odors recruit nearly the same KCs, those with claws from DM1 and DP1m, and the ORN input has the same pairs too close at about half the size: the input holds the seed and the KC stage amplifies it. 1-8ol and ms miss with the opposite sign, too far from every other odor. The two conditions mis-order the same pairs, so that the gap with APL off is mostly the gap with APL on carried over.

**How many KCs each odor recruits is not the lever, and neither is the weighting of the loss.** The per-odor rate oracle scaled each odor's input until its response fraction was the flies': that is worth two to four hundredths of ρ on odor-odor distances at most, and the collapsed pairs stay collapsed, since more KCs are recruited from the same two or three glomeruli. The two-ρ fit put the two ρ's first in the loss, at four times their usual weight: little moved, and no fit raised both (table below). What limits the two ρ's is therefore pairwise structure that the mechanisms in hand do not produce from this input: which KCs an odor recruits, and not how many.

**Searching the weak odors' input within a factor of two raises both ρ's, in one model.** In the weak-input odors' input as a bounded oracle, the ORN response of each of seven weak odors was multiplied, glomerulus by glomerulus, by a factor between one half and two, searched against the two KC matrices themselves. It is an oracle and an in-sample one, so it says what a corrected input could at most buy. Both ρ's rise above the ORN input's own agreement with the KC data, the collapsed pairs open, and the same search on two strongly driven odors as a control gains nothing. The search points at single glomeruli for paa at the recordings' concentration (VL2a up, DP1m and DM4 down), which is a measurement to ask for and not something a fit can decide: the modelled input of paa, euc and -aPine stands in from other concentrations than the KC recordings used.

**None of this moved the APL effect.** The searched input changes which pairs APL moves and not how far (last row of the table). The batched proxy as the block term of a fit, which finished in the same days, showed the same from the other side: the block can be fitted through the trial noise, and the KC–KC inhibition that makes it survive the noise shrinks what APL does to the distances, so the fit went the wrong way on the APL-effect loss. That is where the second goal starts.

## Under the second goal: the main target {#second-goal}

### The mean part: a matter of the reading, and of a few KCs {#mean-part}

**When the goal changed, every model's mean APL effect was between a quarter and a little over half of the flies', and about half of every loss was that shortfall.** It has since been taken apart, and most of it does not belong to the mechanisms.

- **Raw spike counts were being compared with a calcium signal.** A model's distances are cosines on spike counts, the flies' on a nuclear calcium signal that compresses large responses. In the reference model the 1 % of KCs that respond most to an odor hold about two thirds of its summed squared responses, twice the share in the control flies, and they hide what removing APL recruits. Through the calibrated readout the best models' counts give the flies' mean effect (the compressive readout of the model's spike counts).
- **On raw counts about twenty KCs of the real wiring hold the reference model's mean effect down.** They fire twenty or more spikes to some odor with APL on and with APL off, APL does not silence them, and most of them are the cells through which the oracle gain lifts euc and -aPine. The order of the best models on raw counts turns on two to six such cells of one wiring, and the order under the calibrated readout does not (the high-firing KCs that survive APL).
- **A firing ceiling does at the source what the readout does afterwards.** Until then the model's KCs had neither a refractory period nor adaptation, and its hardest-driven KC fired several times more spikes to one odor than whole-cell recordings of KCs show. With a ceiling set from those recordings, raw counts score on the main target as the calibrated readout does, inside the realism bands (the firing ceiling for the KCs).
- **Both remedies cost the block of the models with the oracle gain,** which was carried by the same over-driven cells. A model that is to have the block together with the flies' mean effect needs a carrier of the block that is not a few cells.
- **Fitted directly, the target is matched in its mean and not in its pattern.** The APL-effect fits put the APL-effect loss first in the loss. Under the readout the best of them, the breadth-2 family, has the flies' mean effect, and its loss is nearly all pattern.
- **In the best family the mean rests on two settings, and both have since been taken apart.** One is the breadth weighting of APL: with the weighting removed and the response rates held, the mean effect through the readout falls from {{E89/readings_C.csv:arm=C1;weighting=F_k2;unit=S;readout=cal:mean_effect ~ +.3f}} to {{E89/readings_C.csv:arm=C1;weighting=none;unit=S;readout=cal:mean_effect ~ +.3f}}, for the flies' {{data:apl_effect_mean ~ +.3f}} (the co-response read with a checked estimate and the breadth weighting inside the models' APL). The other is the bouton path: at a tenth of its fitted strength with nothing in its place the mean is {{E95/readings.csv:variant=D0;unit=S;readout=cal:mean_effect ~ +.3f}}. What can hold the mean in its place is an inhibition of the KCs whose amounts are nearly equal among KCs with the same number of claws, or one that follows what each KC loses of its drive; weights made of APL's counted synapses are neither, and lose a part of it ([what the best family's APL is](#family-apl)).
- **The raw-count numbers depend on the length of the odor pulse.** The model's pulse was 0.5 s and the flies' is 2 s. Under the readout the pulse does not matter. On raw counts it does: with a firing ceiling the family keeps its numbers at the flies' pulse only if the ceiling's adaptation outlasts the odor, or with mild depletion at the PN boutons, and without a ceiling only the readout holds at either pulse (the flies' pulse in the model and the remaining candidates for the common error; the flies' recording protocol in the model).
- **Which unit the flies' responses are taken in moves the mean and not the pattern.** In baseline-noise units the flies' mean effect is about a third larger than in the recordings' z-scored units, the pattern across pairs is the same within its floor, and the saved models keep their order. So the mean part is matched against the target as it has been defined so far, and would be open again if the target moved to baseline-noise units (the data side of the main target).

### The pattern part, and the error common to all wirings {#pattern-part}

**The pattern part is a per-odor error and a pair-specific error, and the models are at about twice the data's floor on each.** In the flies most of the effect's variance across pairs follows single odors: an odor's share of the effect follows how much its response fraction rises when APL output is blocked. Setting every odor's response fraction on the flies' does not lower the loss, and the pair-specific part comes with breadth-weighted APL: lowering the breadth exponent trades it against the mean effect and the block (the per-odor profile of the APL effect). By the rules of 2026-11-13 the per-odor part is a lead and not a result, being the part that two halves of the recordings agree on least ([how the main target is read](#reading)).

**The best family owes its standing to the family and not to the hemibrain wiring.** A model has one wiring, while the data are a mean over 11 control recordings minus a mean over 8 recordings with APL output blocked (TNT), from different flies. The APL-effect fit on re-drawn wirings fitted the breadth-2 family again on seven re-drawn wirings. Each ends around the same loss, one parameter set serves all of them, and one wiring's loss carries a term from the draw, {{E72/spread.csv:readout=cal:sd_single ~ .3f}} under the readout, that is as large as the margins between recent fits. ### What the recordings themselves showed {#recordings}

Several of the experiments read recordings and not a model. What they found stands whatever model is used.

- **The unit of the recordings.** Each KC's trace is z-scored over its whole recording, which the experimenter has confirmed, so every response is measured against that KC's own spread and the unit grows with how much the KC responds. Read through the same steps, the saved models keep nearly the order that the calibrated readout gives them, and the flies' responses turn out to grow with the number of odors a KC responds to much more than the models' do (the per-KC normalization of the recordings).
- **Below the response criterion the recordings are not empty.** What is there is mostly signal, and a third of the flies' mean effect comes from products with values below the criterion. With APL off the flies add weak responses where the models add strong ones (the low end of the readout).
- **Two of the 8 recordings with APL output blocked (TNT) stand apart.** In their distances they are stronger and not different, so the mean over all eight stands as the target. Their responses decay about three times faster than the others', and the stimulus record of one of them says that APL was not labelled. Results that depend on it are given against all eight and against the six that are alike (the data side of the main target; the time course of the APL effect in the flies' traces).
- **The odors of one repeat share part of their variability.** Two different odors presented in the same repeat of the panel are more alike than the same two in different repeats. This shared part of the variability is nearly three times larger with APL output blocked (TNT) than in control flies, and most of it is a slow drift over the session. The correction for trial-to-trial noise used until then took the noise of two odors to be independent and put the distances without noise too low; an estimate across repeats replaces it. The main target, the effect as measured, is not touched (the flies' variability across the three repeats).
- **The effect is there from the first second of the odor, and it is largest then.** It does not build up in the window that the target is taken from. The indicator is slow, so a model read as the flies were recorded reads what its plain spike counts at the flies' 2 s pulse read (the time course of the APL effect in the flies' traces).
- **The flies' own responses give the form of APL's inhibition.** An inhibition that grows with the odor's response over all KCs, applied to each recording with APL output blocked (TNT), predicts the control flies' distances on recordings it was not chosen on and leaves a pattern part near the data's floor. An inhibition among KCs of similar tuning is not what the recordings show. This gives a target that needs no control flies and is known about twice as finely as the main target; by itself it is not a screen for the main target (the form of APL's inhibition in the flies' own responses).
- **Under APL the alcohol pairs' co-response rises more in the flies than in the models.** Read like for like, with one estimate on both sides and that estimate's error on known values beside it, the difference holds at every noise level tried (the co-response read with a checked estimate and the breadth weighting inside the models' APL).
- **One fly is about as far from the other flies as the best models are from the data.** Single model flies come to differ from one another as single recordings do only when they are drawn with input flies as well as wirings (the high-firing KCs that survive APL; the ORN input as a population of flies).
- **In wild-type flies the step from claws to somata carries the pattern of the main target,** in flies that share nothing with the recordings that make it ([above](#stages)).
- **APL's own activity in the calyx is for the most part one pattern that all odors share,** with a smaller odor-specific part that repeats between presentations. That part is the odor's: it returns with another vial of the same odor in another block, and not with another odor. A dependence of paa over ms on the depth of the imaged plane, seen in one series, did not return in the next (the APL calyx maps against the connectome; the flies' APL across odors in the held-out vial series).
- **The flies' APL grows with concentration in every odor imaged,** and two vials of one odor differ persistently by more than had been expected ([above](#flies-apl)).

### What is withheld, and which recordings are used {#hold}

Three rules of 2026-11-15 bound what this page can say.

- **A set of connectome tables is on hold.** Tables of APL's synapses, of PN boutons and of KC claws that were derived from four connectome data sets in separate work are not to be used until they are cleared, and a further set of connectome outputs is expected from the source of the tables that the model reads. The earlier version of this page gave two results that rest on them, both from the APL calyx maps against the connectome: one on maps of APL's activity predicted from the connectomes' calyces against the imaged maps, and one on the glomeruli's nearness in the calyx against the common error. Both are withdrawn here, verdicts included, until the readings are made again, and nothing is concluded from them meanwhile. Also withheld are how many of APL's calyx synapses lie outside the claws in the other connectomes, how unequal those counts are there, the experiment on the glomeruli's shares of the claws in other connectomes as a whole, and the maps predicted from the wiring for the weak odors. Every count, run, refit and verdict on this page that involves APL's synapses comes from the hemibrain tables that the model's own loader reads, which are not affected.
- **Only recordings that the experimenter has processed are used.** Recordings that exist only as raw movies are out until further notice, the single fly imaged with all 16 odors of the panel among them.
- **The further recordings of APL were released with a caveat.** The recordings of APL alone beyond the first twelve, and the dual-colour recordings, may be used; the experimenter has not checked their quality, and what was read from them says so.

### Realism {#realism}

Realism is a constraint and not a target, and it has held: the fits of the best family that the numbers on this page come from are inside all four realism bands, on every wiring, and none of them was bought with the response rates. The experiments since 2026-11-13 hold both response rates in every run and report which of their runs fall outside a band; a setting counts as an equal only inside the bands.

What the bands do not see has grown into a list. With APL off the models' responses sit in fewer KCs than the flies': about half of the model KCs respond to none of the 16 odors and too many respond to eight or more. Without a firing ceiling single KCs fire far more spikes to one odor than whole-cell recordings show. Every searched input is an oracle, of the size by which flies differ from one another and in a direction not shown to be the flies'. The family's breadth weighting spreads APL's weight very unequally over the KCs, its exponent being its hardest setting to defend, and no weaker weighting is an equal. Most of its APL's action is at the PN boutons, where the connectome has few of APL's synapses, and for a KC that APL is close to a fixed threshold raise, where the flies' inhibition grows with an odor's response ([above](#family-apl)). Its APL keeps its strength along concentration where the flies' APL loses half of it, and its antennal lobe evens out the odors' total drive, which the flies' does not ([above](#flies-apl)). A third of the wiring's glomeruli have no ORN input; the fuller input gives ten of them measured responses at a small cost on the pattern part. And the pairs with paa, euc and -aPine rest on ORN input measured at another concentration than the KC recordings used.

## What is open {#open}

**Choices about the target and the base model.** These are choices and not results. Each changes what later numbers mean, and none has been made.

- **The unit of the flies' side of the target:** the recordings' z-scored unit, as now, or baseline-noise units, in which the flies' mean effect is about a third larger and the mean part is open again for every model. The pattern part does not depend on it.
- **The reading of the model's side:** plain spike counts at the flies' 2 s pulse, or the calibrated readout. With a firing ceiling whose adaptation outlasts the odor the two give one number. The choice now also bears on what the family's feedback is worth, since what the feedback adds over a fixed inhibition per KC shows through the readout and not on raw counts.
- **Whether the firing ceiling joins the base model, and with which adaptation.** With the indicator's kinetics as measured in the recordings the shorter adaptation costs little more than one draw of the wiring moves the loss; what the flies' traces do ask is that late firing with APL off be bounded, which the family without a ceiling does not do.
- **Which recordings make the target:** all eight with APL output blocked (TNT), or the six that are alike. This waits on the experimenter.
- **Whether a KC's signal saturates with its firing.** The reading of the models as the recordings are normalized has a saturating map and a linear one. With the first the model's somata without APL are the flies', with the second they are too far apart, so that the choice decides whether anything is asked of the model's responses without APL.
- **Whether the fuller input becomes the input of the base model.** For it, measured responses stand where there were zeros. Against it stand about two thousandths of the pattern part, inside the margin of an equal, by which every later comparison would shift.
- **Whether a limit on the bouton share replaces the bound on the APL→bouton scale, and at which price:** {{diff:E94/d_scores.csv:unit=S:pattern_all -- E94/d_scores.csv:unit=S:pattern_all_unchanged ~ .4f}} of the pattern part with the family's own weights, {{diff:E95/f_scores.csv:unit=S:pattern_all -- E95/f_scores.csv:unit=S:pattern_all_unchanged ~ .4f}} with weights that reach every claw alike, which the connectome does not have, or {{diff:E96/d_scores.csv:unit=S:pattern_all -- E96/d_scores.csv:unit=S:pattern_all_unchanged ~ .4f}} with counted synapses, and a part of the mean with it. The bound itself costs nothing at fixed scales.
- **Whether two readings of the flies get a place in fits or stay tests:** the step from claws to somata in wild-type flies, and the target that needs no control flies, which the form of APL's inhibition in the flies' own responses gave. Until then both are given beside the main target.
- **Whether the base model's antennal lobe changes.** At fixed scales the flies' fall of APL with concentration has a price on the pattern part and on the KCs' responses at the weakest inputs; the family fitted again will say what is left of the first.
- **Whether searched inputs read on a single wiring count in the progress plot's line of ρ on the per-pair APL effect.**
