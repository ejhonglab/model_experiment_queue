%%% hover
APL effect: Of one odor pair, its odor-odor distance with APL on minus its distance with APL off. In the KC data, the control flies' distance minus the distance with APL output blocked (TNT), each the mean over recordings of the cosine distance; in a model, its distance with APL on minus its distance with APL acutely off.
APL-effect loss: The root mean squared difference between a model's and the KC data's per-pair APL effect over the 120 odor pairs; lower is better. It splits exactly into a mean part and a pattern part, and its floor is the loss that a second sample of the same flies would show against the first.
mean part: The part of the APL-effect loss that comes from the model's mean APL effect over the 120 pairs differing from the flies'.
pattern part: The APL-effect loss with both means removed: how far the model's pattern of the APL effect across the 120 pairs is from the flies', whatever the two means are.
common error: The part of the best family's pattern error that every wiring shares: the per-pair APL effect of the mean over eight wirings minus the KC data's, each side's mean removed, under the calibrated readout. In it the model's APL effect is too small among 2h, IaA, pa, eb, ep and ms and too large among 1-8ol, 1-5ol, 1-6ol and 6al.
calibrated readout: Each model spike count n replaced by n / (n + k) before the distances are taken, with k set on the model's counts with APL on so that an odor's response is spread over the responding KCs as in the control flies. It compresses large counts as a calcium indicator does and leaves which KCs respond, and every response rate, unchanged.
firing ceiling: In a model, a refractory period after each spike together with an adaptation that raises the KC's threshold at every spike and decays slowly, which bounds how many spikes a KC fires to one odor.
realism bands: The four ranges that a model has to stay inside for its loss to count: its response rate with APL on, the ratio of its response rates with APL off and on, its response rate ρ with APL on, and its distribution of the number of odors each KC responds to, each set from the recordings.
baseline-noise units: A KC's response divided by the standard deviation of its own signal within the pre-odor windows of its recording. Unlike the recordings' z-scored unit, it does not grow with how much the KC responds. The realism bands count the flies' responses in these units.
oracle: A model manipulation that is handed part of the answer (which glomeruli or which KCs to change, read off the KC data or off the model's output scored against them). A ceiling test, never a mechanism.
breadth-2 family: The best family on the main target: APL inhibition weighted by how broadly each KC is driven (breadth exponent 2), KC–KC inhibition, no per-claw threshold and no glomerulus gain, fitted to the APL effect at the control flies' response rate.
block: The fruity × small-ester block: the nine pairs of a fruity ester (2h, IaA, pa) with a small ester (2-but, eb, ep), which the control flies' KCs hold further apart than the ORN input predicts and which close when APL output is blocked.
co-response: Of two odors, how many times more often than chance a KC that responds to one responds to the other.
draw of the wiring: One re-drawn version of the PN→KC wiring (a permutation of claws within KC types, or a shuffle matched to the real wiring on every glomerulus pair's count of shared KCs). The standard deviation of one family's loss over such draws, each with its own fit, is the yardstick for whether two fits differ.

## In brief {#brief}

**Since the main goal became the APL effect on odor-odor distances, pair by pair, the mean of that effect has been accounted for and its pattern across pairs has not. The models' mean effect was too small mostly because raw spike counts were being compared with a calcium signal, and because about twenty over-driven model KCs dominated the distances: read through a compressive readout, or with a ceiling on how many spikes a KC fires, the best family has the flies' mean effect inside the realism bands. What is left is an error in the pattern that every wiring shares, too little APL effect among the esters and too much among the alcohols, well above the floor that the recordings' own variability sets. Every kind of change tried against it has failed to carry more than a few thousandths of it to wirings and recordings that the change was not fitted to: APL's weight by glomerulus, the input, the transfer at the claw, the timing, the differences between flies, and the form and the weighting of APL's inhibition. The lead that remains is in what the models' KCs do without APL, and how much a fitted change can be shown to carry is limited by the number of recordings. Under the goal before it, the fit of the two distance matrices, the gap was traced to a few odors whose modelled input collapses onto the same KCs, which an ORN measurement at the recordings' concentrations can settle and a fit cannot.**

- **The mean of the APL effect is matched, the pattern is not.** The table under [where the main target stands](#standing) gives both for the models as they stood when the goal changed and for the best family now.
- **The best family is a family and not one wiring.** Fitted again on seven re-drawn wirings it ends around the same loss, and what all eight wirings get wrong is the same.
- **Nothing tried since carries that common error.** Fourteen kinds of change are listed under [what was tried against the common error](#attempts), each with what it showed.
- **The recordings were read more closely than before,** and several of the findings are about the data and not the model: the unit of the responses, two recordings that stand apart, variability shared by the odors of a repeat, the time course of the effect, and the form of inhibition that the flies' own responses imply. They are under [what the recordings themselves showed](#recordings).
- **Four choices about the target and the base model are open,** and several questions are with the experimenter. They are under [what is open](#open).

## Under the first goal: where the gap in ρ on odor-odor distances lies {#first-goal}

**The gap is on a few odors, and not where the fits had been scored.** ρ on odor-odor distances is one number over 120 pairs, and the per-pair decomposition of the gap split one minus ρ exactly into a share for each pair and each odor. The nine block pairs and the pairs within a chemical group, on which the fits until then had been scored, carry a tenth of the gap. Three odors carry two thirds of it: paa, B-myr and 2-but, which every model puts far too close to one another. The reference model has paa and B-myr at a cosine distance of {{E61/worst_pairs.csv:short=reference 40 Hz;condition=on;pair=paa / B-myr:model_distance ~ .2f}} with APL on, where the control flies have {{E61/worst_pairs.csv:short=reference 40 Hz;condition=on;pair=paa / B-myr:data_distance ~ .2f}}, nearly orthogonal. The three odors recruit nearly the same KCs, those with claws from DM1 and DP1m, and the ORN input has the same pairs too close at about half the size: the input holds the seed and the KC stage amplifies it. 1-8ol and ms miss with the opposite sign, too far from every other odor. The two conditions mis-order the same pairs, so that the gap with APL off is mostly the gap with APL on carried over.

**How many KCs each odor recruits is not the lever, and neither is the weighting of the loss.** The per-odor rate oracle scaled each odor's input until its response fraction was the flies': that is worth two to four hundredths of ρ on odor-odor distances at most, and the collapsed pairs stay collapsed, since more KCs are recruited from the same two or three glomeruli. The two-ρ fit put the two ρ's first in the loss, at four times their usual weight: little moved, and no fit raised both (table below). What limits the two ρ's is therefore pairwise structure that the mechanisms in hand do not produce from this input: which KCs an odor recruits, and not how many.

**Searching the weak odors' input within a factor of two raises both ρ's, in one model.** In the weak-input odors' input as a bounded oracle, the ORN response of each of seven weak odors was multiplied, glomerulus by glomerulus, by a factor between one half and two, searched against the two KC matrices themselves. It is an oracle and an in-sample one, so it says what a corrected input could at most buy. Both ρ's rise above the ORN input's own agreement with the KC data, the collapsed pairs open, and the same search on two strongly driven odors as a control gains nothing. The search points at single glomeruli for paa at the recordings' concentration (VL2a up, DP1m and DM4 down), which is a measurement to ask for and not something a fit can decide: the modelled input of paa, euc and -aPine stands in from other concentrations than the KC recordings used.

**None of this moved the APL effect.** The searched input changes which pairs APL moves and not how far (last row of the table). The batched proxy as the block term of a fit, which finished in the same days, showed the same from the other side: the block can be fitted through the trial noise, and the KC–KC inhibition that makes it survive the noise shrinks what APL does to the distances, so the fit went the wrong way on the APL-effect loss. That is where the second goal starts.

## Under the second goal: the main target {#second-goal}

### The mean part: a matter of the reading, and of a few KCs {#mean-part}

**When the goal changed, every model's mean APL effect was between a quarter and a little over half of the flies', and about half of every loss was that shortfall.** It has since been taken apart, and most of it does not belong to the mechanisms.

- **Raw spike counts were being compared with a calcium signal.** A model's distances are cosines on spike counts, the flies' on a nuclear calcium signal that compresses large responses. In the reference model the 1 % of KCs that respond most to an odor hold about two thirds of its summed squared responses, twice the share in the control flies, and they hide what removing APL recruits. Through the calibrated readout the best models' counts give the flies' mean effect (the compressive readout of the model's spike counts).
- **On raw counts about twenty KCs of the real wiring hold the reference model's mean effect down.** They fire twenty or more spikes to some odor with APL on and with APL off, APL does not silence them, and most of them are the cells through which the oracle gain lifts euc and -aPine. The order of the best models on raw counts turns on two to six such cells of one wiring, and the order under the calibrated readout does not (the high-firing KCs that survive APL).
- **A firing ceiling does at the source what the readout does afterwards.** Until then the model's KCs had neither a refractory period nor adaptation, and its hardest-driven KC fired several times more spikes to one odor than whole-cell recordings of KCs show. With a ceiling set from those recordings, raw counts score on the main target as the calibrated readout does, inside the realism bands (the firing ceiling for the KCs).
- **Both remedies cost the block of the models with the oracle gain,** which was carried by the same over-driven cells. A model that is to have the block together with the flies' mean effect needs a carrier of the block that is not a few cells.
- **Fitted directly, the target is matched in its mean and not in its pattern.** The APL-effect fits put the APL-effect loss first in the loss. Under the readout the best of them, the breadth-2 family, has the flies' mean effect, and its loss is nearly all pattern.
- **The raw-count numbers depend on the length of the odor pulse.** The model's pulse was 0.5 s and the flies' is 2 s. Under the readout the pulse does not matter. On raw counts it does: with a firing ceiling the family keeps its numbers at the flies' pulse only if the ceiling's adaptation outlasts the odor, or with mild depletion at the PN boutons, and without a ceiling only the readout holds at either pulse (the flies' pulse in the model and the remaining candidates for the common error; the flies' recording protocol in the model).
- **Which unit the flies' responses are taken in moves the mean and not the pattern.** In baseline-noise units the flies' mean effect is about a third larger than in the recordings' z-scored units, the pattern across pairs is the same within its floor, and the saved models keep their order. So the mean part is matched against the target as it has been defined so far, and would be open again if the target moved to baseline-noise units (the data side of the main target).

### The pattern part, and the error common to all wirings {#pattern-part}

**The pattern part is a per-odor error and a pair-specific error, and the models are at about twice the data's floor on each.** In the flies most of the effect's variance across pairs follows single odors: an odor's share of the effect follows how much its response fraction rises when APL output is blocked. Setting every odor's response fraction on the flies' does not lower the loss, and the pair-specific part comes with breadth-weighted APL: lowering the breadth exponent trades it against the mean effect and the block (the per-odor profile of the APL effect).

**The best family owes its standing to the family and not to the hemibrain wiring.** A model has one wiring, while the data are a mean over 11 control recordings minus a mean over 8 recordings with APL output blocked (TNT), from different flies. The APL-effect fit on re-drawn wirings fitted the breadth-2 family again on seven re-drawn wirings. Each ends around the same loss, one parameter set serves all of them, and one wiring's loss carries a term from the draw, {{E72/spread.csv:readout=cal:sd_single ~ .3f}} under the readout, that is as large as the margins between recent fits. Since then two fits that differ by about that much are compared on the same re-drawn wirings, and families are compared as means over wirings, the counterpart of data that are a mean over flies. The mean over the eight wirings has a lower loss than the real wiring alone, all of the gain in the pattern part.

**What remains is the same on every wiring.** Under the readout the APL effect of the breadth-2 family is too small among 2h, IaA, pa, eb, ep and ms, where the flies have the block, and too large among 1-8ol, 1-5ol, 1-6ol and 6al. This common error is in almost every saved model, and it is the target of everything in the next subsection. Its pattern part stands at {{E72/population.csv:group=all:cal_pattern ~ .3f}} against a floor of {{E66/data_floor_parts.csv:data=graded;stat=apl_effect_rmse_centered:mean ~ .3f}} (lower is better).

**The one change that holds where it was not fitted is small.** The input searched on the real wiring alone, at 0.6 of its size, lowers the pattern part on four wirings and on the half of the recordings that its search never saw. The second split of the flies moves only four of the 19 recordings to the other side, and one of its two inputs gains while the other loses: what a fitted input gains on recordings it has not seen is in part a statement about which recordings were held out.

**So what limits a fitted change is the recordings.** With the 8 recordings with APL output blocked (TNT) split in two, the halves differ from each other by more than a fitted input moves the model, and a flexible fit follows the split. More recordings would raise what a fit can be shown to carry; the recordings of the panel that the target does not use are the nearest, and they wait on the experimenter's check of their quality.

**Where the lead is now.** Every reading through the trial noise points to who responds and not to how strongly. The ester pairs' error goes with how large a share of an odor's responders APL removes, the alcohol pairs' with whether the responders that survive APL are shared more often than before. The second is a firm difference between the best family and the flies: under APL the alcohols' co-response rises more in the flies than in the family, at every noise level tried. The KCs that the model's APL removes although a cut by amplitude would keep them are its broad, many-claw KCs. With static forms of inhibition and the breadth weighting both closed on APL's side, what is left is a mechanism between the claw and the soma that changes which KCs respond without APL.

### What the recordings themselves showed {#recordings}

Several of the experiments read the KC recordings and not a model. What they found stands whatever model is used.

- **The unit of the recordings.** Each KC's trace is z-scored over its whole recording, which the experimenter has confirmed, so every response is measured against that KC's own spread and the unit grows with how much the KC responds. Read through the same steps, the saved models keep nearly the order that the calibrated readout gives them, and the flies' responses turn out to grow with the number of odors a KC responds to much more than the models' do (the per-KC normalization of the recordings).
- **Below the response criterion the recordings are not empty.** What is there is mostly signal, and a third of the flies' mean effect comes from products with values below the criterion. With APL off the flies add weak responses where the models add strong ones (the low end of the readout).
- **Two of the 8 recordings with APL output blocked (TNT) stand apart.** In their distances they are stronger and not different, so the mean over all eight stands as the target. Their responses decay about three times faster than the others', and the stimulus record of one of them says that APL was not labelled. Results that depend on it are given against all eight and against the six that are alike (the data side of the main target; the time course of the APL effect in the flies' traces).
- **The odors of one repeat share part of their variability.** Two different odors presented in the same repeat of the panel are more alike than the same two in different repeats. This shared part of the variability is nearly three times larger with APL output blocked (TNT) than in control flies, and most of it is a slow drift over the session. The correction for trial-to-trial noise used until then took the noise of two odors to be independent and put the distances without noise too low; an estimate across repeats replaces it. The main target, the effect as measured, is not touched (the flies' variability across the three repeats).
- **The effect is there from the first second of the odor, and it is largest then.** It does not build up in the window that the target is taken from. The indicator is slow, so a model read as the flies were recorded reads what its plain spike counts at the flies' 2 s pulse read (the time course of the APL effect in the flies' traces).
- **The flies' own responses give the form of APL's inhibition.** An inhibition that grows with the odor's response over all KCs, applied to each recording with APL output blocked (TNT), predicts the control flies' distances on recordings it was not chosen on and leaves a pattern part near the data's floor. An inhibition among KCs of similar tuning is not what the recordings show. This gives a target that needs no control flies and is known about twice as finely as the main target; by itself it is not a screen for the main target (the form of APL's inhibition in the flies' own responses).
- **One fly is about as far from the other flies as the best models are from the data.** Single model flies come to differ from one another as single recordings do only when they are drawn with input flies as well as wirings (the high-firing KCs that survive APL; the ORN input as a population of flies).
- **APL's own activity in the calyx,** imaged for three odors, is for the most part one pattern that all odors share, with a smaller odor-specific part that repeats between presentations (the APL calyx maps against the connectome).

### Realism {#realism}

Realism is a constraint and not a target, and it has held: the fits of the best family that the numbers on this page come from are inside all four realism bands, on every wiring, and none of them was bought with the response rates. Three things the bands do not see are the same in every best fit. With APL off the models' responses sit in fewer KCs than the flies': about half of the model KCs respond to none of the 16 odors and too many respond to eight or more. Without a firing ceiling single KCs fire far more spikes to one odor than whole-cell recordings show. And every searched input is an oracle, of the size by which flies differ from one another and in a direction not shown to be the flies'. The pairs with paa, euc and -aPine rest on ORN input measured at another concentration than the KC recordings used.

## What is open {#open}

**Choices about the target and the base model.** Four things are choices and not results, and each changes what later numbers mean.

- **The unit of the flies' side of the target:** the recordings' z-scored unit, as now, or baseline-noise units, in which the flies' mean effect is about a third larger and the mean part is open again for every model. The pattern part does not depend on it.
- **The reading of the model's side:** plain spike counts at the flies' 2 s pulse, or the calibrated readout. With a firing ceiling whose adaptation outlasts the odor the two give one number, so this weighs only if the ceiling stays out.
- **Whether the firing ceiling joins the base model, and with which adaptation.** The case for an adaptation that outlasts the odor is weaker than it first looked, since with the indicator's kinetics as measured in the recordings the shorter adaptation costs little more than one draw of the wiring moves the loss; what the flies' traces do ask is that late firing with APL off be bounded, which the family without a ceiling does not do.
- **Which recordings make the target:** all eight with APL output blocked (TNT), or the six that are alike. This waits on the experimenter.

**Questions for the experimenter.**

- What was different about the two strongest recordings with APL output blocked (TNT), in the cross, the indicator, the age of the flies, the preparation or the imaging, and whether APL was labelled in the one whose record says that it was not.
- The quality check of the recordings of the panel that the target does not use. More recordings are what would raise how much a fitted change can be shown to carry.
- ORN responses at the KC recordings' concentrations, first DL1's response to ms and DM6's and VL2a's to paa at 10^-3, which would decide whether those odors' deficit is in the input.
- For the APL calyx maps, their orientation, depth and pixel size, and maps of eb or 2h against 1-6ol or 1-8ol, which would show whether APL's activity differs in place between the two groups of the common error.
- How long a KC's threshold stays raised after it has fired, which is what a firing ceiling whose adaptation outlasts the odor would have to be defended with.

**Work in hand.**

- **What the reduced searched input is.** Six inputs have now been searched on the real wiring. How far they agree with one another, glomerulus by glomerulus, and whether they share entries that a measurement could test, is the last part of the controls and the replication of the reduced searched input. Its code is written and its 16 model runs have not been started.
- **The breadth weighting's result is not yet in the summary.** The experiment finished on the morning of 2026-11-13; its place among the completed experiments and its passage in the summary are still to be written.
- **The next mechanism.** A change between the claw and the soma that alters which KCs respond without APL, read on two targets: what the flies' form of inhibition makes of the flies' responses without APL, and the main target for what the model's own APL then makes of them. The alcohols' co-response under APL is reported beside the main target for whatever is tried.
