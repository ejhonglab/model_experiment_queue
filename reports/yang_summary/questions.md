## noise-floor-method
asked: 2026-11-16
sentence: From the three trials of each odor, the correlation between two independent trial means of the same odor is the noise ceiling (0.77 in control flies, 0.81 with APL output blocked (TNT); between single trials 0.56 and 0.61).
sentence_main: From the three trials of each odor, the distance between two independent trial means of the same odor is the noise floor (0.23 in control flies, 0.19 with APL output blocked (TNT); between single trials 0.44 and 0.39).
question: Is the method to calculate the cosine distance between every pair of trials of the same odor, average across the 3 pairs (since there are 3 repeats), then average across odors?

Not quite. That is how the other number in the sentence is made, the one between single trials ({{inv:data:ctrl_single_trial_floor ~ .2f}} in control flies and {{inv:data:tnt_single_trial_floor ~ .2f}} with APL output blocked (TNT)). The {{inv:data:ctrl_trial_mean_floor ~ .2f}} and {{inv:data:tnt_trial_mean_floor ~ .2f}} are not measured from pairs of trials: they are an estimate of the correlation that two independent three-trial means of one odor would have.

**The value between trial means**, per recording and odor (X is 3 trials × KCs):

1. Take the trial mean x̄ over the 3 trials, and its squared norm |x̄|².
2. Take the noise power s²: each KC's variance across the 3 trials (n − 1 in the denominator), summed over KCs.
3. Averaging 3 trials with independent noise leaves s²/3 of noise power in |x̄|², so the signal power is |a|² = |x̄|² − s²/3.
4. Two independent three-trial means share the signal a and each carries its own noise of power s²/3, so their expected cosine similarity is |a|² / |x̄|². The main page gives the same thing as a distance, (s²/3) / |x̄|².
5. Average over the 16 odors within a recording, then over the recordings (11 control, 8 with APL output blocked (TNT)).

It has to be an estimate: only 3 trials exist, and they cannot be split into two separate three-trial means.

**The value between single trials** is the one described in the question: the cosine similarity of each of the 3 pairs of trials of an odor, averaged over the 3 pairs, then over the odors of a recording, then over recordings.

Two cautions on the trial-mean value. It assumes that the noise of the 3 trials of one odor is independent, and it is the ratio of two expected values, an approximation of the expected cosine. Code: `analyse` in kcq/noise_floor.py; the registry entries are made in kcq/data_targets.py.

On the main page these are distances: {{data:ctrl_trial_mean_floor ~ .2f}} and {{data:tnt_trial_mean_floor ~ .2f}} between trial means, {{data:ctrl_single_trial_floor ~ .2f}} and {{data:tnt_single_trial_floor ~ .2f}} between single trials.

## earlier-correction-too-low
asked: 2026-11-16
sentence: the same by the earlier correction, which takes the noise of two odors to be independent (too high)
sentence_main: the same by the earlier correction, which takes the noise of two odors to be independent (too low)
question: "Too low" means the corrected pair distance is too low? And the noise is not independent because?

Yes. On the main page the row is a mean pair distance, and the earlier correction put it too low. Here the same row is a mean pair correlation, so the earlier correction put it too high: it removed more than the trial noise.

| statistic | control flies | APL output blocked (TNT) |
|---|---|---|
| mean pair correlation as measured, on the three-trial means | {{inv:data:ctrl_mean_dist ~ .2f}} | {{inv:data:tnt_mean_dist ~ .2f}} |
| without the trial noise, by the estimate across repeats | {{inv:data:noise_free_mean_dist_ctrl ~ .2f}} | {{inv:data:noise_free_mean_dist_tnt ~ .2f}} |
| without the trial noise, by the earlier correction (too high) | {{inv:data:ctrl_mean_dist_noise_corrected ~ .2f}} | {{inv:data:tnt_mean_dist_noise_corrected ~ .2f}} |

**Why the noise of two odors is not independent.** In the recordings, the responses to two different odors presented in the same repeat of the panel are more alike than the responses to the same two odors in different repeats. Between single trials the correlation of two different odors is {{inv:E81/distances_by_kind.csv:units=z;values=graded;genotype=TNT3c;level=genotype;pairset=all;kind=same_repeat:value ~ .3f}} in the same repeat against {{inv:E81/distances_by_kind.csv:units=z;values=graded;genotype=TNT3c;level=genotype;pairset=all;kind=different_repeat:value ~ .3f}} in different repeats in control flies, and {{inv:E81/distances_by_kind.csv:units=z;values=graded;genotype=TNT;level=genotype;pairset=all;kind=same_repeat:value ~ .3f}} against {{inv:E81/distances_by_kind.csv:units=z;values=graded;genotype=TNT;level=genotype;pairset=all;kind=different_repeat:value ~ .3f}} with APL output blocked (TNT). With independent noise the two would be equal. The difference, the same-repeat gap, is {{data:repeat_gap_ctrl ~ .3f}} and {{data:repeat_gap_tnt ~ .3f}}, and it is above zero in every recording.

So part of a KC's trial-to-trial variability is common to the odors of a repeat: {{E81/variance_parts.csv:units=z;genotype=TNT3c;level=genotype;cls=responders;statistic=share_shared:value ~ .0%}} of the trial-to-trial variance of a responding KC's response in control flies and {{E81/variance_parts.csv:units=z;genotype=TNT;level=genotype;cls=responders;statistic=share_shared:value ~ .0%}} with APL output blocked (TNT). About three quarters of the gap follows the time between the two presentations ({{E81/order_gap_decomposition.csv:units=z;basis=positions;genotype=TNT3c;statistic=separation_share:value ~ .0%}} and {{E81/order_gap_decomposition.csv:units=z;basis=positions;genotype=TNT;statistic=separation_share:value ~ .0%}}), mostly a slow drift over the session, and the rest is a step tied to the repeat itself. The page measures this and does not say what in the fly or the preparation causes it.

**Why that biases the earlier correction.** It divided the product of the two odors' trial means, x̄ₐ·x̄ᵦ, by the two signal norms (|x̄|² − s²/3 for each odor), on the assumption that noise adds nothing to x̄ₐ·x̄ᵦ. The two trial means are taken over the same three repeats, so the noise the two odors share within each repeat does add to that product. The correction counted it as similarity between the odors while taking all the noise out of the norms. The corrected correlation comes out too high, most with APL output blocked (TNT), where the shared part is larger, and the mean APL effect too high with it.

**What replaced it.** The estimate across repeats uses only products of trials from different repeats, which the shared part does not enter: the cosine between single trials of the two odors from different repeats, divided by the geometric mean of the two odors' cosines between their own trials across the same repeats. The section on the flies' variability across the three repeats has the details ([there](#repeat-structure)).

## orn-kc-level-comparison
asked: 2026-11-16
sentence: With APL output blocked (TNT), twice as many KCs respond to each odor (0.150 instead of 0.068 of the KCs, with the per-odor pattern conserved), and the mean pair correlation rises from 0.16 to 0.34.
sentence_main: With APL output blocked (TNT), twice as many KCs respond to each odor (0.150 instead of 0.068 of the KCs, with the per-odor pattern conserved), and the mean pair distance falls from 0.84 to 0.66.
question: I think this might not be a fair comparison: the ORNs use a dense code with about 50 units, whereas the KC space is sparse and high dimensional. Using cosine to measure the correlation of the ORN representations and comparing between the two spaces doesn't seem fair. Am I wrong?

No, the comparison with the ORN input is not a fair one, and it does not hold once both sides are measured the same way. Two details differ from the question, and they make the point stronger. The ORN values on this page are Pearson correlations across the 36 glomeruli imaged in both ORN panels, not cosine similarities. The KC values are cosine similarities across all KCs of a recording. The clause the question is about, "above the ORN input's own", therefore set two different measures of two different codes side by side; it has since been removed from the sentence, on the main page and here.

| mean pair correlation (120 pairs) | ORNs | KCs, control flies | KCs, APL output blocked (TNT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| as on this page: Pearson for the ORNs, cosine similarity for the KCs | {{inv:data:orn_mean_dist ~ .2f}} | {{inv:data:ctrl_mean_dist ~ .2f}} | {{inv:data:tnt_mean_dist ~ .2f}} |
| Pearson correlation for all three | {{inv:data:orn_mean_dist ~ .2f}} | {{inv:data:ctrl_mean_dist_pearson ~ .2f}} | {{inv:data:tnt_mean_dist_pearson ~ .2f}} |
| cosine similarity for all three | {{inv:data:orn_mean_dist_cosine ~ .2f}} | {{inv:data:ctrl_mean_dist ~ .2f}} | {{inv:data:tnt_mean_dist ~ .2f}} |

**With one measure on both sides, the KCs stay less correlated than the ORN input even with APL output blocked (TNT)**: 0.24 against 0.29 by Pearson, 0.34 against 0.65 by cosine. "Above the ORN input's own", as the sentence ended when the question was asked, was true only of the mixed pairing in the first row.

**Why the measure matters so much for the ORNs.** The ORN code is dense and positive: 96 % of the glomerulus responses are above zero. Every pair of odors then shares a common positive part, which the cosine counts as similarity and the Pearson correlation removes. The KC responses are sparse and z-scored, centred near zero, so the two measures nearly agree in control flies and differ more with APL output blocked (TNT), where twice as many KCs respond.

**Levels are not comparable across the two stages in any case.** The level of a correlation depends on how dense the code is, on the number of units and on noise. Trial noise lowers the KC values (without it, by the estimate across repeats, {{inv:data:noise_free_mean_dist_ctrl ~ .2f}} and {{inv:data:noise_free_mean_dist_tnt ~ .2f}}), and the ORN vectors are means over flies, which carry less of it.

**What can be compared.** Control flies against APL output blocked (TNT): the same kind of cells and the same measure, and the mean pair correlation about doubles. And the order of the pairs, which no change of scale touches: ρ on odor-odor correlations with the ORNs is {{data:rho_ctrl_orn ~ .2f}} in control flies and {{data:rho_tnt_orn ~ .2f}} with APL output blocked (TNT). The page's statistics are of these two kinds.

The Pearson values for the KCs and the cosine value for the ORNs are now on the main page too, as distances: the second row of its table gives the mean pair distance with one measure on both sides, and the paragraph after the sentence asked about says how to read it. On this page the same row and paragraph give them as correlations.

## isotonic-r2-at-ceiling
asked: 2026-11-16
sentence: The order of pairs is largely kept (ρ=0.87 on odor-odor correlations between the two KC matrices) and the relation between them is monotone (isotonic R² 0.94, as high as the measurement ceiling allows).
sentence_main: The order of pairs is largely kept (ρ=0.87 on odor-odor distances between the two KC matrices) and the relation between them is monotone (isotonic R² 0.94, as high as the measurement ceiling allows).
question: Does it mean the isotonic fit is perfect, and what is left is purely experimental noise?

Not quite. It means that, over all 120 pairs together, a monotone curve explains about as much of the control matrix as measurement noise leaves room for. It does not mean that every residual is noise: the nine fruity × small-ester pairs deviate from the curve together, and that deviation is the block.

**What "the ceiling" is here.** Two halves of the recordings of one genotype differ only by noise, so the isotonic R² between their mean matrices is what a perfectly monotone relation would give at that amount of data. The page gives no number for it; computed for this answer over 300 random splits:

| isotonic R² between mean matrices | mean | 5th to 95th percentile |
|---|---|---|
| two halves of the control recordings (noise only) | {{lit:0.96 # isotonic R² between the mean cosine matrices of random halves of the 11 control recordings, 300 splits, computed 2026-11-16 in viki's session with kcq.harness.isotonic_resid for Yang's question; not a registry entry}} | {{lit:0.95 to 0.98 # the same 300 splits, 5th and 95th percentiles}} |
| two halves of the recordings with APL output blocked (TNT) (noise only) | {{lit:0.91 # isotonic R² between the mean cosine matrices of random halves of the 8 TNT recordings, 300 splits, computed 2026-11-16 in viki's session for Yang's question; not a registry entry}} | {{lit:0.84 to 0.96 # the same 300 splits, 5th and 95th percentiles}} |
| half of the control recordings on half of those with APL output blocked (TNT) | {{lit:0.91 # isotonic R² of the mean matrix of a random half of the control recordings on that of a random half of the TNT recordings, 300 splits, computed 2026-11-16 in viki's session for Yang's question; not a registry entry}} | {{lit:0.84 to 0.95 # the same 300 splits, 5th and 95th percentiles}} |
| all control recordings on all with APL output blocked (TNT), the page's value | {{data:iso_r2 ~ .2f}} | {{data:iso_r2.lo ~ .2f}} to {{data:iso_r2.hi ~ .2f}} |

At equal amounts of data the fit across the two genotypes is as good as the fit between two halves of the noisier genotype. In that sense no large part of the control matrix is left for a non-monotone effect of APL.

**Why the leftover is still not purely noise.** The same splits give the mean residual of the nine block pairs:

| how far the nine block pairs sit below the fit, on average | mean | 5th to 95th percentile |
|---|---|---|
| two halves of the control recordings (noise only) | {{lit:0.000 # mean isotonic residual of the nine fruity x small-ester pairs between random halves of the control recordings, 300 splits, computed 2026-11-16 in viki's session for Yang's question}} | {{lit:−0.013 to +0.012 # the same 300 splits, 5th and 95th percentiles, sign as on this page (fit minus measured)}} |
| two halves of the recordings with APL output blocked (TNT) (noise only) | {{lit:−0.008 # the same between random halves of the TNT recordings, 300 splits}} | {{lit:−0.058 to +0.031 # the same 300 splits, 5th and 95th percentiles}} |
| control flies on APL output blocked (TNT), the block | {{data:block ~ +.3f}} | {{data:block.lo ~ +.3f}} to {{data:block.hi ~ +.3f}} |

Noise alone leaves these nine pairs on the curve on average. Between the genotypes all nine sit below it, by more than noise gives. They are 9 of the 120 pairs and hold roughly a quarter of the variance the fit leaves.

**Two limits of the statement.** A step function through 120 points can follow some of the noise, so its R² flatters the fit, and a small pair-specific effect spread over a few pairs could hide inside the noise. That is why the residual is read for a group of nine pairs and not pair by pair ([how the monotone fit is done, and why](#fn-isotonic)). And monotone is weaker than uniform: any effect of APL that depends only on how correlated a pair is without APL is absorbed by the curve, whatever its shape.

When the question was asked this heading read "What blocking APL output does, mostly: a uniform sparsening", which was worded the wrong way round, and the reading in the question is the right one. It now reads "What blocking APL output does, mostly: it undoes a uniform sparsening", on the main page as here, and the sentence after it says that it is about the flies with APL output blocked (TNT). Blocking APL output sparsens nothing: with APL output blocked (TNT) twice as many KCs respond to each odor ({{data:tnt_frac ~ .3f}} of the KCs against {{data:ctrl_frac ~ .3f}}) and the mean pair correlation rises from {{inv:data:ctrl_mean_dist ~ .2f}} to {{inv:data:tnt_mean_dist ~ .2f}}. What the heading names is what APL's output does in the intact fly, which is what blocking it takes away.

**In control flies APL sparsens and decorrelates.** Fewer KCs respond to each odor, and every one of the 120 pairs is less correlated than it is with APL output blocked (TNT): the per-pair APL effect runs from {{data:apl_effect_min ~ +.2f}} to {{data:apl_effect_max ~ +.2f}}, {{data:apl_effect_mean ~ +.2f}} on average. The two are not opposites. With fewer KCs responding, two odors share fewer KCs, so the sparsening is the way the inhibition decorrelates.

**What "uniform" adds.** It does not say that every pair is decorrelated by the same amount. It says that one rule holds for all pairs: how much APL decorrelates a pair depends only on how correlated the pair is without APL, not on which two odors they are. That is what the monotone fit of the next sentence tests (isotonic R² {{data:iso_r2 ~ .2f}}).

**What "mostly" leaves out.** The nine fruity × small-ester pairs, which APL decorrelates more than that rule gives: the block, in the paragraph that begins "The one pair-specific exception: the block" ([which pairs deviate from the fit, and how consistently](#yq-pairs-off-the-monotone-fit)).

APL's four sets of weights are synapse counts of the hemibrain, each scaled to a mean of 1 and multiplied by one gain. APL sums the drive that is left after its own inhibition, so it is a feedback loop, and it is driven by the input to the claws and not by the KCs' spikes.

**What is tuned.** Two numbers, on all 16 odors. First the constant of the somatic threshold, with APL's weights at zero, so that {{lit:10 % # the tuning target with APL's weights at zero: TARGET = 0.05 in experiments/E3_drive_dependent_gain.py times p.kc.sp_factor_pre_apl = 2.0 in olfsysm/libolfsysm/src/olfsysm.cpp, read 2026-11-17}} of the KC × odor entries respond. Then APL's one gain, until {{lit:5 % # the tuning target with APL on: TARGET = 0.05 in experiments/E3_drive_dependent_gain.py, read 2026-11-17}} respond. APL off is the same model with APL's output weights set to zero and nothing tuned again, so 10 % respond. The saved run has {{apl_effect/by_variant.csv:folder=E13_bouton_coincidence;tag=bouton_base:rate_on ~ .3f}} with APL on and {{apl_effect/by_variant.csv:folder=E13_bouton_coincidence;tag=bouton_base:rate_off ~ .3f}} with APL off.

**Where the scheme is more exact than the sentence it is attached to.**

- **Boutons and claws.** The saved runs have {{E94/a1_path.csv:wiring=real:n_boutons ~ .0f}} boutons and {{E94/a1_path.csv:wiring=real:n_claws ~ ,.0f}} claws ({{E13/stats_variants.csv:bouton_base:mean_claws_per_kc ~ .1f}} per KC), and the sentence now gives these counts; when the question was asked it said 443 boutons and about 9,500 claws.
- **Glomeruli without data.** {{lit:18 # the glomeruli without ORN input among those with claws in results/E29_oracle_substrate/glomerulus_substrate.csv, counted by reports/yang_model_scheme.py}} of the wiring's glomeruli have no measured ORN response: for every odor their ORNs stay at the spontaneous rate.
- **The threshold.** It is not one number for all KCs: each KC's threshold is the tuned constant plus twice the KC's summed claw input before the odor.
- **The spike count.** It is taken over the whole simulated window, from {{lit:0.5 s before the odor's onset to 0.25 s after its end # p.time.start = -0.5, p.time.end = 0.75 and p.time.stim.end = 0.5 in olfsysm/libolfsysm/src/olfsysm.cpp, read 2026-11-17; the KCs are simulated from p.time.start and their spikes are summed over that window}}, not over the odor alone.

**Where the later changes of this page act on the scheme.**

- The per-claw threshold: between steps 4 and 5, on each claw's drive before the KC sums its claws.
- Breadth-weighted APL: the APL→claw weights of step 4, multiplied for each KC by a power of how broadly it responds.
- Inhibition among KCs: added at step 5.
- Glomerulus-specific claw strength: the bouton→claw weight of step 4, for the claws of eight glomeruli.
- APL driven by the KCs' spikes: APL's input, in place of the claws' drive.
- The firing ceiling: step 5, a refractory period and an adaptation of the threshold.
