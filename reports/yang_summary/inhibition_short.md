# Inhibition in the model and the KC code, against the flies: the short version

written 2026-11-17 for Yang; last model experiment: {{meta:last_experiment}}, built: {{meta:generated}}

This page takes one question out of the all-experiments summary: what each way of building inhibition into the model does to the KC code, and how close that brings the code to the flies'. Every value for a pair of odors is a correlation, the cosine similarity of the two odors' KC responses, as in Yang's version of the full page, which has the methods and every experiment.

## In brief

- **The baseline's inhibition makes the code sparse and hardly decorrelates it.** One APL with the connectome's weights halves the number of responding KCs, as in the flies, but lowers the mean correlation between odors far less than the flies' APL does.
- **Which KCs APL inhibits is what matters.** Inhibiting the broadly responding KCs hardest triples the decorrelation and brings the code with APL on close to the flies' level. No other change to APL does this.
- **Inhibition among KCs adds agreement on which odors are alike.** On top of the weighting by breadth it gives the best agreement with the control flies' matrix. It stays when APL is removed, so too few KCs are released without APL unless a fit rebalances it.
- **Where APL acts decides how many KCs APL holds back.** Moving inhibition to the PN boutons gives the flies' doubling of the response rate without APL; alone it does not change which odors are alike.
- **The form of the inhibition changes little.** Division or subtraction, one APL or local compartments, claw by claw or summed over the KC: the code stays the same.
- **No implementation reaches the flies' pattern.** The best model has nearly the flies' mean decorrelation and about two thirds of the agreement that the recordings allow on which pairs APL decorrelates most.

- **Mostly it is the same for all pairs.** The order of the pairs is kept (ρ={{data:rho_ctrl_tnt ~ .2f}} on odor-odor correlations between the two KC matrices), and a monotone curve from one matrix to the other explains {{data:iso_r2 ~ .2f}} of the variance, which is as much as the measurement allows.
- **Every odor loses the same share of its responders.** Without APL {{data:rate_ratio_fraction ~ .1f}} times as many KCs respond, whatever the odor.

## The inhibition of the baseline model

![The baseline model (the bouton model). Top: the chain from ORNs to the read-out, with the number of units at each stage, the wiring between stages, the LNs of the antennal lobe and APL. Below: the transformation at each stage as the simulator computes it, APL's own equation, and the two numbers that are tuned.](results/summary_yang/model_scheme.png)

The baseline has one APL. It sums what is left of the drive of all claws and all PN boutons, and one value is subtracted from every bouton and every claw, each weighted by its synapse count in the hemibrain. Two numbers are tuned: the KCs' threshold, so that a tenth of the KC × odor entries respond without APL, and APL's gain, so that a twentieth respond with it. APL off is the same model with APL's output set to zero.

- **Breadth-weighted APL.** It is the only change to APL that decorrelates. The mean APL effect triples, the correlation with APL on comes close to the flies', and the agreement with the control flies' matrix rises a little.
- **Site, algebra and drive of APL.** More inhibition at the boutons, division in place of subtraction and APL driven by the KCs' spikes leave the two matrices where the baseline has them. With division APL's effect is smaller and its pattern further from the flies'.
- **Local APL.** With {{lit:529 # the main page, "What did not matter, and what that rules out", the local APL units experiment}} APL compartments from the connectome in place of one APL, the odor-odor correlations are those of the baseline (ρ={{lit:0.997 to 0.999 # the main page, "What did not matter, and what that rules out", the local APL units experiment: ρ on odor-odor distances between the model with local units and the model with one APL}} between the two models): each KC averages over claws that lie all through the calyx.
- **Evenness across odors.** Driven by the KCs' spikes, APL takes nearly the same share of every odor's responders, as in the flies. The spread of that share across the 16 odors is {{E35/stats_variants.csv:sub_thr70_spk:suppression_sd_log ~ .2f}}, against {{E35/stats_variants.csv:sub_thr70:suppression_sd_log ~ .2f}} with APL driven by the claws' drive, both with the threshold at each claw (flies: {{lit:0.15 # the main page's table in "What drives APL: the per-odor suppression", the KC data's spread of the suppression across the 16 odors; not a registry entry}}).
- **Inhibition among KCs.** Alone it lowers the APL effect, because it stays when APL is removed. With breadth-weighted APL it gives the best agreement with the control flies' matrix and the flies' correlation with APL on. In that row removing APL releases too few KCs: {{E8:breadth3_tau0.05_kckc100:rate_ratio_off_over_on ~ .1f}} times as many respond, against the flies' {{data:rate_ratio_fraction ~ .1f}}.
- **The threshold at each claw.** It decorrelates the code with and without APL, below the flies' level without APL, and it lowers the agreement with both KC matrices.

The error is the root mean squared difference between the model's and the flies' APL effect over the 120 pairs. Its floor is the error that a second sample of the same flies would show against the first, so nothing below it is worth reaching.

- **What the breadth weighting is worth in a fit.** With everything else fitted, it takes the correlation with APL on to the flies' level, lifts the agreement with the control flies' matrix and cuts the error on the APL effect by a third.
- **The fits move APL's inhibition to the PN boutons.** They put it at about three times the baseline's strength there, which is what gives the flies' ratio of response rates. With it held at the baseline's strength, only {{E31/fits.csv:thr70_k2_aplpnfixed1:fit_ratio ~ .1f}} times as many KCs respond without APL.
- **The best model.** It is the breadth-weighted family with a bound on the KCs' firing, which is a change to the KCs and not to the inhibition. It has nearly the flies' mean APL effect and the lowest error. Its correlations are above the flies' in both conditions, and its agreement on which pairs APL decorrelates most is about two thirds of what the recordings allow.

## Other forms of inhibition tried in the best family

None lowers the error on the APL effect.

- **APL's inhibition summed over a KC's claws**, in place of taken from each claw separately: level.
- **A gain on each claw's drive**, in place of the subtraction: worse.
- **A fixed raise of each KC's threshold while APL is on**, in place of the feedback: nearly level. For this target the family's APL is close to a threshold difference per KC between the two conditions, and the feedback itself adds little.
- **A weight of APL that differs by glomerulus**: helps the pairs it was fitted on and not the pairs of an odor that was left out.
- **The connectome's APL synapses on the KCs outside the claws**, in the place of the inhibition at the boutons: loses a part of the mean APL effect.
- **Inhibition from the lateral horn's centrifugal neurons**: none of the flies' structure appears.
- **A weaker normalization in the antennal lobe**: a small price on the APL effect.

## What is still wrong

- **The pattern.** On every wiring the model's APL effect is too small among 2h, IaA, pa, eb, ep and ms and too large among 1-8ol, 1-5ol, 1-6ol and 6al.
- **Probably not the form of APL.** Read on the flies' own responses, the inhibition that turns the responses without APL into the control flies' grows with the odor's response over all KCs and only weakly with a KC's breadth. Put into the model it does no better than the model's APL, so the error that is left is read as lying in which KCs respond without APL.
- **The input for five odors.** ms, B-myr, euc, paa and 1-8ol drive few glomeruli in the model's ORN input and recruit too few KCs without APL, which no inhibition repairs.
- **The realism of the inhibition that fits.** Of the drive that APL takes from the claws, the best family takes {{E94/a1_path.csv:wiring=P2:bouton_share ~ .2f}} to {{E94/a1_path.csv:wiring=real:bouton_share ~ .2f}} at the PN boutons, where the connectome has about a tenth of APL's calyx synapses ({{E94/a2_connectome.csv:set=calyx, all:share_pn ~ .3f}} to {{E94/a2_connectome.csv:set=calyx, assigned, KCs with a type:share_pn ~ .3f}}).
- **What would build the breadth weighting in the fly.** It is a rule of the model, and the connectome's synapse counts, which the baseline uses, do not give it. Two candidates are in the literature. A KC is predicted to inhibit itself through APL more than it inhibits another KC, because APL's activity stays local (Amin et al., 2020), so a KC that responds more would be inhibited more. And a KC's inhibition may be matched to its excitation as the circuit adapts (Abdelrahman, Vasilaki and Lin, 2021). Neither has been tested against these recordings.
